# Alpha 2.2 — Chaos Engine

This update continues the existing Alpha 2.1 game and its playtest follow-up. The attached assignment was treated as project requirements, with the user's three design decisions taking priority. This report describes the verified Alpha 2.2 build. Publication status is recorded in the GitHub commit history.

## What changed

**Destruction with causes.** Unlimited Smash has greater mass and a bounded contact impulse, while its damage multiplier is lower. It can displace intact architecture instead of requiring every piece to fracture. Piercer is narrow, concentrates damage and retains reduced incoming momentum after breaking a support, for up to three contacts. Burst applies distributed, shield-attenuated force over a 145-unit radius. Fragments inherit linear/angular motion and conserved mass. Gravity, crushing, material inertia and support failure remain real; there are no height bonuses or scripted collapse timers.

**Vertical construction without banning bunkers.** Three seeded starter silhouettes provide scaffold floors, a partial bridge or an incomplete keep. Both sides get mirrored geometry. Tower frame and Raised deck each place three independent load-bearing timbers in a single gesture. They share preview/rotation geometry but have no invisible welds or floating anchors. Troops can occupy their floors and fall when supports disappear. Low shelter and dense, distributed construction remain viable; no minimum height or immunity was added. CPU builders use the assemblies and can relocate a soldier onto an upper floor.

**A repair queue with decisions.** Each round deals both players the same seeded six-piece sequence. Only the current piece is available; the next is previewed. A beam and brick precede one volatile crate or breakaway post, and basic utility pieces follow it. Geometric pieces vary. There is at most one explosive crate, never a sequence of forced explosives. Pieces fit within the smallest territory's dimensions. Crowded wreckage can still prevent a particular placement, so Skip always advances at a cost of two seconds, without a limited-discard dead end. Six skips cost at most 12 of the shortest 24-second repair window. Invalid placement and cancelled gestures consume nothing. CPU uses the same queue, collision rules and skip cost.

**Reactive objects and arenas.** The Flats provides the clean baseline. Powder Yard adds mirrored powder crates. Hanging Works adds a heavy weight, a visible rope and a clearly marked, ground-fixed breakaway post per side. Arena anchors/weights cannot be dragged out of their mechanisms during construction. A support's destruction removes its actual Matter constraint and wakes the suspended body. The weight's next consequences come from gravity and collisions. Ordinary breakaway posts can also arrive as dynamic repair supports. Existing terrain variants remain available.

**Understandable chains.** `reactive.js` supplies shared visual identities and damage reactions. Hard damage (10 units from a single event) or exhausted health arms a volatile detonation only when battle physics is active. Explosions use the same `hurt()` pipeline, so an explosion can damage a post, another crate or a soldier without pair-specific scripts. A deduplicated blast queue preserves newly scheduled reactions across steps. Constraint links are removed generically when either linked body disappears. Bounded diagnostic history records impacts, penetration, support breaks, rope releases and blast sources. Twenty seconds of armed idling in every new arena/starter combination causes no reactions or soldier damage.

**Touch and readability.** Soldiers support tap-select/tap-place as well as dragging, with real floor checks and forgiving snapping. Two-finger takeover still cancels placement/firing safely. The aiming arc stops at 75% of the predicted ground-flight samples and fades over its final quarter. The close-view target inset no longer draws an arc; no exact-impact marker replaces it. Both-fort framing remains the default. Travel stays at the slower 1.2× playback from the previous feedback pass and destruction at 1×. Blast rings communicate the affected area. New controls stay in the existing contextual tray; narrow phones use two rows of initial pieces to retain 44px targets.

## Reference review

Reviewed the supplied PlushSiege `world-physics.ts`, `physics.ts` and damage/effects approaches. Adopted the principles of real body constraints, removal-triggered release, deferred/deduplicated explosive chains, bounded debris and momentum-preserving fracture. The existing contact-space impact adaptation remains attributed. No campaign, asset collection, weather, zones, progression or spring system was imported. Reign of Doom retains its own match, renderer, controls and code organization.

## Limits and next human playtests

**Actual iPad hardware and iPad Safari were not tested.** WebKit was unavailable: its pinned download was rejected by network policy with HTTP 403 at `cdn.playwright.dev` and `playwright.download.prss.microsoft.com`. The not-run result is explicit and is not counted as a pass. Cloud Chromium touch tests cannot establish Safari compatibility, sustained iPad frame rate, thermal behavior or multi-user ergonomics.

The automated scenarios establish physical causes and functional choices, not fun or competitive equilibrium. Some machine matches still reached the existing ceiling, and some aggressive large-scale matches ended before repairs. This variation needs real playtests rather than another paper balance claim. Discarding costs and reactive-piece frequency are conservative starting values, not proven enjoyable values. Exact cross-browser replay identity is not guaranteed by Matter.js.

Recommended next session:

1. Two unfamiliar players on a landscape iPad: start, build upward, tap-place soldiers, shoot, repair and rematch without coaching. Test the short Safari toolbar layout, device rotation, background/resume, two fingers and second-finger rotation while holding.
2. Alternate bunker and tower strategies across ten paired matches on The Flats. Compare exposed soldiers, useful floor locations, collapse readability and whether either style becomes the automatic choice.
3. Play Powder Yard and Hanging Works. Ask players what will happen before hitting the crate or post, then compare that expectation with the physical result. Watch whether rope release and secondary impacts remain legible at both-fort zoom.
4. Judge the six-piece queue under real repair pressure. Try isolation, offensive traps and skips; check whether two seconds feels like a dilemma or a penalty. Record placements, skips and unused time.
5. Rate Smash satisfaction without kills, Piercer precision, Burst displacement, follow camera and full collapse viewing. Try repeated rounds with rubble; measure actual iPad frame rate and match duration.

No new ammunition types, materials, troop classes, economy or progression systems were added. Springs and broader mechanism authoring were deferred to keep the three reactive behaviors complete and connected to ordinary physics.
